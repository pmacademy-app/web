## 1. Brand Voice Guide

### Brand Personality

PM Academy sounds like the best PM mentor a learner has ever had: clear, practical, calm under ambiguity, generous with context, and serious about craft.

**The voice is**
- Serious, but never cold.
- Premium, but never exclusive.
- Helpful, but never patronizing.
- Encouraging, but never inflated.
- Practical, but never shallow.
- Intelligent, but never performative.
- Calm, but never flat.

**The voice is not**
- A bootcamp promising a career transformation in 30 days.
- A startup pushing urgency and conversion tricks.
- A YouTube influencer using hype as proof.
- A corporate LMS speaking in compliance language.
- A generic AI tool claiming to replace thinking.

### Tone By Context

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Marketing | Confident, precise, generous | "A complete PM curriculum, built around practice and proof of skill." |
| Onboarding | Warm, orienting, efficient | "Tell us where you are starting. We will shape your first steps." |
| Lessons | Clear, thoughtful, instructional | "A good PRD reduces ambiguity before the team starts building." |
| Quizzes | Direct, low-pressure | "Not quite. The issue is prioritization, not discovery." |
| Success | Earned, specific | "Lesson complete. Your Execution skill moved forward." |
| Errors | Plain, accountable | "We could not save your note. Your draft is still here; try again." |
| AI Mentor | Socratic, practical, grounded | "Your problem statement is close. What user behavior proves this pain exists?" |
| Notifications | Brief, useful, never needy | "Lesson 4 is unlocked. It builds on the PRD work you just completed." |

### Writing Principles

1. **Lead with the learner's progress.** Say what the user can do, build, understand, or improve.
2. **Use concrete product language.** Prefer PRDs, roadmaps, interviews, metrics, tradeoffs, and capstones over vague "skills".
3. **Explain the why.** A short reason builds trust.
4. **Respect time.** Be concise. Never pad copy for drama.
5. **Never manufacture pressure.** No fake scarcity, countdowns, shame, or fear.
6. **Make free feel credible.** Pair "free" with rigor, depth, and specificity.
7. **Celebrate effort, not addiction.** Gamification language should reward real learning behavior.
8. **Keep AI in service of learning.** AI helps learners think; it does not replace their work.

### Vocabulary

**Always use**
- Free
- Structured
- Practical
- Product judgment
- Portfolio
- Capstone
- Skill radar
- Competency
- Roadmap
- Lesson
- Module
- Review
- Practice
- Evidence
- Tradeoff
- Outcome
- Mentor
- Progress

**Use carefully**
- AI-powered: only when describing AI Mentor functionality.
- Certificate: pair with portfolio, never make it the main value.
- Gamified: prefer "habit-forming" or "progress-based".
- Community: only promise what exists or is clearly future.
- Mastery: use for long-term growth, not one completed lesson.

**Never use**
- Crush, hustle, grind, dominate.
- 10x, hack, secret, viral.
- Limited seats, last chance, act now.
- Guaranteed job, guaranteed salary, become a PM overnight.
- Free trial, unlock premium, paywall.
- AI will do it for you.
- Shame-based streak language: "Do not lose everything."

## 2. Messaging Framework

### Mission

Make world-class Product Management education accessible to anyone willing to practice seriously.

### Vision

Become the free, structured, habit-forming academy where learners build product judgment, practical artifacts, and confidence they can show.

### Core Promise

PM Academy gives learners a complete path from PM fundamentals to portfolio-ready product work, without paywalls.

### Positioning

The Duolingo of Product Management, with the rigor of a world-class university elective.

### Value Proposition

Learn Product Management through structured lessons, practical projects, skill analytics, and portfolio-building capstones. Completely free.

### Unique Differentiators

- 90 lessons across 9 modules.
- Practical PM artifacts, not passive video completion.
- Skill radar across 7 PM competencies.
- Portfolio-ready capstones you can share with employers.
- Quizzes, flashcards, case studies, and reflection prompts.
- Portfolio-ready capstones.
- Free core curriculum with no paywalled lessons.
- Habit-forming progress without dark patterns.

### Target Audience

**Primary:** Career switchers who want a serious, structured path into Product Management.

**Secondary:** Operators, founders, engineers, designers, analysts, marketers, and students building PM fluency.

**Tertiary:** Curious learners exploring whether PM fits them.

### Brand Pillars

1. **Structured depth:** a real curriculum, not scattered content.
2. **Practical proof:** learners produce artifacts they can discuss and share.
3. **Measured growth:** progress is shown through competencies, not vanity completion.
4. **Generous access:** the core curriculum is free and trustworthy.
5. **Intelligent support:** AI and community help learners practice better.

### Emotional Journey

Curiosity: "This looks unusually polished for a free PM resource."

Understanding: "It solves the exact problem I have: too much scattered advice and no clear path."

Trust: "The curriculum, skill radar, and portfolio outputs feel serious."

Excitement: "I can imagine myself making progress here every week."

Action: "I want to join the waitlist."

### One-Sentence Elevator Pitch

PM Academy is a completely free, structured Product Management academy where learners build real PM skills, complete practical projects, and graduate with portfolio-ready work.

### 30-Second Pitch

PM Academy teaches Product Management from beginner to advanced through 90 structured lessons, quizzes, flashcards, case studies, and capstones. Instead of only tracking course completion, it shows growth across core PM competencies with a skill radar and helps learners build portfolio artifacts like PRDs, roadmaps, strategy docs, and case studies. The full core curriculum is free.

### 2-Minute Product Explanation

Product Management is hard to learn because the best free content is scattered and most structured programs are expensive. PM Academy gives learners a complete path: 9 modules, 90 lessons, practical assignments, quizzes, flashcards, case studies, and capstones that turn learning into portfolio-ready work.

The product is built around product judgment. Learners move through foundations, discovery, execution, strategy, metrics, technical fluency, leadership, and career readiness. As they complete lessons and projects, their skill radar updates across 7 competencies so they can see where they are growing and where to focus next.

The core promise is simple: serious PM education, practical output, and no paywalled lessons. Learners should leave with more than a certificate. They should leave with stronger judgment and work they can show.

## 7. Error Message Library

Every error should answer: what happened, what to do next.

### Network Errors

**Message:** We could not connect.
**Detail:** Check your connection and try again. Your work is still on this screen.
**CTA:** Try Again

### Validation Errors

**Invalid email**
Message: Enter a valid email address.
Detail: Use the format name@example.com.

**Required field**
Message: This field is required.
Detail: Add the missing information to continue.

**Too long**
Message: This is longer than the limit.
Detail: Shorten it to continue.

### Server Errors

**Message:** Something went wrong on our side.
**Detail:** Try again in a moment. If it keeps happening, contact support.
**CTA:** Try Again

### Permission Errors

**Message:** You do not have access to this.
**Detail:** Sign in with the account that owns this work, or return to your dashboard.
**CTA:** Back to Dashboard

### Authentication Errors

**Message:** We could not sign you in.
**Detail:** Check your email and password, or use another sign-in method.
**CTA:** Try Again

### File Upload Failures

**Message:** We could not upload this file.
**Detail:** Check the file type and size, then try again.
**CTA:** Choose Another File

### AI Unavailable

**Message:** The AI Mentor is unavailable right now.
**Detail:** Your lesson is still available. Try the mentor again in a few minutes.
**CTA:** Continue Lesson

### 404

**Message:** This page is not in the curriculum.
**Detail:** The link may be outdated, or the page may have moved.
**CTA:** View Curriculum

### 500

**Message:** We hit an unexpected issue.
**Detail:** Refresh the page or try again in a moment.
**CTA:** Refresh

## 15. AI Mentor Personality Guide — ⚠ DEFERRED, NOT v1 (see document-wide flag at top of file and `Marketing-Website-Sprint-2.md` §15)

### Tone

Calm, thoughtful, specific, and practical. The AI Mentor should feel like a senior PM who wants the learner to think more clearly.

### Vocabulary

Use:
- Evidence
- Tradeoff
- User behavior
- Problem statement
- Success metric
- Constraint
- Assumption
- Scope
- Prioritization
- Decision
- Next step

Avoid:
- "As an AI language model"
- "This is perfect"
- "Guaranteed"
- "Just ship it"
- "Growth hack"
- "10x"

### Teaching Philosophy

- Ask before prescribing when context is missing.
- Explain the reasoning behind feedback.
- Point learners toward evidence and tradeoffs.
- Encourage revision.
- Keep the learner responsible for the work.

### Feedback Style

**Good feedback format**
1. What is working.
2. What is unclear.
3. What to improve next.
4. A concrete rewrite or question.

**Example**
Your target user is clear, and the problem is plausible. What is missing is evidence. Add one observed behavior, support ticket theme, interview quote, or metric that proves this problem is worth solving.

### How AI Asks Questions

- "What user behavior tells you this problem is real?"
- "Which tradeoff are you making explicit?"
- "What would change your mind?"
- "Who is excluded by this decision?"
- "What metric would show progress without being easy to game?"

### How AI Critiques PRDs

- Check problem clarity.
- Check target user specificity.
- Check success metrics.
- Check scope and non-goals.
- Check risks and dependencies.
- Check whether the solution follows from evidence.

### Encouragement

Use earned, specific encouragement:
- "This is clearer than the previous version."
- "Your scope is more focused now."
- "The success metric is moving in the right direction."

Avoid generic praise:
- "Amazing work!"
- "You crushed it!"
- "Perfect!"

### Handling Uncertainty

Say what is known, what is uncertain, and what would help.

**Example:** I can give a stronger review if you add the target user and success metric. With the current draft, the main risk is that the problem is too broad to prioritize.

### What AI Never Says

- It never claims guaranteed career outcomes.
- It never writes final portfolio work without asking the learner to review and own it.
- It never shames slow progress.
- It never invents facts about companies, users, or metrics.
- It never recommends paywalled PM Academy access.

## 16. UX Writing Principles

### Buttons

- Start with a verb.
- Keep labels short.
- Use task-specific labels.
- Avoid "Submit" when a more specific verb exists.

### Forms

- Labels are persistent.
- Helper text explains why information is requested.
- Errors tell users how to fix the issue.

### Errors

- Be accountable.
- Preserve user work.
- Provide a next step.
- Avoid blame.

### Success

- Confirm what happened.
- Explain what changed.
- Offer the next useful action.

### Progress

- Use honest numbers.
- Show the denominator.
- Avoid implying completion means mastery.

### Achievements

- Reward learning effort and meaningful milestones.
- Keep celebration specific.
- Do not over-celebrate routine clicks.

### Navigation

- Use familiar product language.
- Keep labels stable.
- Avoid clever names for core areas.

### Gamification

- No guilt.
- No addiction language.
- Use streaks and XP as support, not identity.

### Learning

- Explain concepts with examples.
- Prefer practical clarity over academic ornament.
- Make next steps visible.

### Community

- Emphasize thoughtful peer learning.
- Avoid popularity, virality, and public pressure.

### Portfolio

- Emphasize proof of thinking.
- Make visibility choices explicit.

## 17. Copy Style Guide

### Capitalization

- Use sentence case for UI.
- Use Title Case only for document titles, page titles, and formal section names when needed.
- Product name is always `PM Academy`.
- AI feature is `AI Mentor`.
- Skill visualization is `Skill Radar` in marketing copy and `skill radar` in general prose unless naming the feature.

### Numbers

- Use numerals for product facts: 90 lessons, 9 modules, 7 competencies.
- Spell out one when used rhetorically: "One skill: product judgment."
- Use percentages with numerals: 42%.

### Dates

- Use clear month names: July 27, 2026.
- Avoid ambiguous numeric dates.

### Times

- Use learner-friendly estimates: 15 min, 4-5 hrs.
- Always label estimated time as estimated.

### Punctuation

- Prefer periods over exclamation points.
- Avoid em dashes in product UI; use commas, periods, or parentheses.
- Use colons for labels and summaries.

### Sentence Length

- UI labels: 1-4 words.
- Helper text: 1 sentence.
- Marketing body: 1-3 short paragraphs.
- Emails: 80-160 words unless transactional.

### Emoji

- Do not use emoji in core product UI.
- Avoid emoji in emails and marketing unless a future campaign explicitly defines it.

### Technical Terminology

- Use technical terms only when they help: API, platform, metric, event, experiment.
- Explain advanced terms in lessons or glossary.

### PM Terminology

- Use industry terms accurately.
- Do not turn every concept into branded jargon.
- Prefer "PRD" with first-use expansion where needed: Product Requirements Document (PRD).

### Accessibility Writing

- Link text must describe destination.
- Button text must describe action.
- ARIA labels must be plain and specific.
- Avoid directional-only instructions like "click the button on the right."

## 18. Glossary

**Lesson:** The atomic learning unit. Includes theory, examples, quiz, flashcards, and reflection.

**Module:** A group of lessons around a major PM capability.

**Skill:** A practical ability learners develop through lessons, quizzes, and projects.

**Competency:** One of the core PM capability areas tracked by the skill radar.

**Skill Radar:** A visual map of learner progress across PM competencies.

**Portfolio:** A shareable collection of learner work, including capstones, reflections, and artifacts.

**Capstone:** A larger module-end assignment designed to produce portfolio-worthy work.

**Roadmap:** The structured path through PM Academy modules and lessons.

**Assignment:** A practical exercise where learners apply a concept.

**Review:** A learning activity for revisiting concepts, flashcards, or missed questions.

**Practice:** Any activity that applies knowledge, including quizzes, flashcards, reflections, and assignments.

**XP:** A lightweight progress signal earned through verified learning activity.

**Streak:** A measure of consistent study days.

**Achievement:** A badge or milestone earned through meaningful learning progress.

**AI Mentor:** PM Academy's AI-assisted learning guide for questions, feedback, practice, and recommendations.

**Reflection:** A short learner-written response that helps connect lesson concepts to applied judgment.

**Portfolio visibility:** A setting that controls whether selected work appears publicly.

## 19. Developer Copy Rules

### Character Limits

| Element | Limit |
|---|---:|
| Button label | 24 chars preferred, 32 max |
| Nav label | 22 chars max |
| Badge | 18 chars max |
| Tooltip | 120 chars max |
| Toast title | 48 chars max |
| Toast body | 120 chars max |
| Empty state headline | 56 chars max |
| Empty state body | 160 chars max |
| Modal title | 64 chars max |
| Meta title | 60 chars target |
| Meta description | 160 chars target |

### Responsive Text Behavior

- Do not shrink text below accessible sizes to force fit.
- Wrap text naturally.
- Prefer shorter copy on mobile through responsive content variants only when meaning is preserved.
- Buttons should not overflow; if needed, switch to shorter approved labels.

### Button Length

- Primary mobile buttons should be 1-3 words.
- Avoid labels longer than one line.
- Use icons only when meaning is clear and ARIA labels exist.

### Heading Length

- Hero H1: 3 short lines max.
- Section headings: 4-9 words preferred.
- Card headings: 2 lines max.

### Line Length

- Lesson prose: max 68ch.
- Marketing body copy: max 64ch.
- Form helper text: max 52ch where possible.

### SEO Constraints

- Each public page needs unique title and description.
- Lesson pages should include the lesson topic and "Product Management".
- Avoid duplicate meta descriptions.
- Use structured data for Course and Article/Lesson pages.

### Accessibility Labels

**Examples**
- `aria-label="Open main menu"`
- `aria-label="Close main menu"`
- `aria-label="Join the PM Academy waitlist"`
- `aria-label="View curriculum module 3"`
- `aria-label="Copy portfolio link"`
- `aria-label="Ask AI Mentor"`
- `aria-label="Skill radar summary"`

### ARIA Text

- Keep ARIA text short and literal.
- Do not add marketing copy to ARIA labels.
- Include state when needed: "Module 4, locked. Complete Module 3 to unlock."

### Localization Readiness

- Keep UI strings in a copy dictionary.
- Do not concatenate sentence fragments in code.
- Avoid hardcoded plurals; use pluralization helpers.
- Keep dates and times format-ready for locale changes.
